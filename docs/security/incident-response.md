# Incident response runbook

**Operational draft. Belgian legal/privacy review required before public beta for regulatory decisions.** Appoint incident lead, security owner, controller decision maker, counsel and processor contacts before hosted operation. This is not a claim that every security incident is a reportable personal-data breach.

1. Detect and triage: correlate the random request ID, status spikes, rate-limit events, missing-object/cleanup-task age, authorization alerts and infrastructure signals. Record awareness time and facts in a restricted incident record. Never copy raw property files or extracted text into routine logs or general issue trackers.
2. Contain: restrict affected API/storage access, revoke compromised token rows, isolate affected workers, stop ingestion or external processors if relevant. Preserve necessary evidence in a separate controlled location with an owner and expiry. Maintain deletion-task integrity; avoid destroying evidence before scope is established.
3. Assess affected data: identify whether confidentiality, integrity or availability of personal data was compromised; determine workspaces, documents, third-party categories, access windows, encryption state and evidence of access/exfiltration. Minimise investigative copies and preserve source/correlation provenance.
4. Assess affected people: consider buyers, sellers, agents, notaries, syndics, contractors and co-owners; distinguish data subjects from account owners. Assess probable severity and likelihood of harm. Do not assume the buyer can waive other people's rights.
5. Coordinate: involve controller/counsel and affected infrastructure/scanning/AI processors or subprocessors. Obtain access records, containment confirmation, deletion/retention details and cross-border scope through approved incident channels. No autonomous software messaging or mailbox access is introduced.
6. Regulatory assessment: under GDPR Article 33, assess notification to the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of controller awareness of a personal-data breach, unless unlikely to risk people's rights/freedoms. Record decisions and reasons for delay; provide phased information where appropriate. Counsel confirms competence, facts and obligations. See [official GDPR Articles 33–34](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) and the [Belgian DPA](https://www.dataprotectionauthority.be/citizen).
7. Data-subject assessment: separately consider whether high risk requires communication to affected people and whether a relevant exception applies. Counsel approves scope, channel and clear remedial advice. A security alert alone does not automatically trigger every notification.
8. Remediate/recover: fix authorization/provider/storage weaknesses; rotate affected credentials; validate isolation; replay pending purge tasks; confirm processor containment; verify backup recovery will not resurrect deleted data. Reopen affected processing only after the incident lead approves operational safeguards.
9. Postmortem: record cause, detection gaps, impact/notification decisions, containment and remediation evidence, owners/dates and residual risks. Update tests, processor registry, DPIA, notices and this runbook. Retain minimal incident evidence only under a separately documented purpose/basis and expiry.

No regulatory reporting or third-party notification is sent automatically. Breach assessment, communications and legal sign-off remain human operational responsibilities.
